# Default On

2026-11-05 · @Albert Armea

## Concept

**A 1970s Saturday-morning civics cartoon that teaches kids how an opt-out works, and fails.** A cheerful Narrator walks a small cartoon toggle switch named Little Switch through a field trip of real products. Every time Switch reaches OFF, a hand from off-screen flips it back to ON.

- **Why this format:** this week Google told schools that Search and AI are now inseparable. A children's educational song, about a company deciding what goes into children's education, makes that point without a lecture.
- **The hook:** "Default on." Every story in the song follows the same three beats: it arrives without asking, the off switch is buried or greyed out or resets, and the company calls it more choice.
- **The turn:** the Narrator starts as the company's friendly voice and ends up unsure of his own script. Switch, who only ever wanted to rest, gets the last word.
- **The ending shows the seams:** a "summary of this song" box pops up over the final chorus, and the viewer can't close it.

Working title: **Default On**. Alternates: *Little Switch*, *How an Opt-Out Works*.

## Style prompt and generation notes

**Swung**** 1970s ****jazz-folk ****educational**** ****cartoon ****song****, about 1****00**** BPM**** shuffle, sung like a lesson told by a friendly older neighbour.** The model is the classic three-minute civics and grammar shorts: small jazz combo, conversational vocals, spoken asides over a vamp, a weary bluesy character verse, and a chorus that teaches a word. No show or artist names in the prompt, since generators block or ignore them.

```
1970s educational cartoon song, swung jazz-folk shuffle, small jazz combo, Fender Rhodes and upright piano, walking upright bass, brushed drums, flute and muted trumpet fills, relaxed raspy conversational male vocal, sincere young character vocal, spoken asides over a vamp, half-time bluesy character verse, scat fills, sing-along teaching chorus, warm analog tape, slightly tinny mono mix
```

- **Settings:** 100 BPM, swung 4/4, B-flat major, about 3:00. Set vocal language to English.
- **What changed from the jingle version:** no kazoo, tambourine or children's-choir polish. Swing, Rhodes, walking bass and talk-sung phrasing do the work. If a take comes back as bright kids' pop, add "jazz trio, swing feel, conversational" and remove "sing-along."
- **Voices:** Narrator (relaxed, slightly raspy, half-spoken) and Switch (earnest, young, a little wistful). Lines are written for one voice at a time. If the generator merges them, try a female voice for Switch.
- **Switch's pre-chorus is the blues moment.** It drops to half-time, like the character songs in the old shorts. If the generator won't change feel, generate it as its own section and splice it in.
- **Lyrics field:** paste with section tags only. Remove the bracketed voice notes, or keep a short `[Switch]` / `[Narrator]` tag if the generator respects it.
- **The [click] marks** are sound cues, not lyrics. Leave them out of the generator and add a real switch click in the edit.
- **Bridge:** the record scratch and rewind are added in the edit. Generate the bridge straight, then cut.
- **Keep every take with its prompt**, as in the other songs. The take log goes in the repo.

## Video sketch and running gags

**Look: limited-animation 1970s cartoon** with loose ink lines, watercolour backgrounds that repeat on loops, film grain and a slightly wobbly gate. Switch is a cream-coloured wall switch with stubby arms and big eyes; the Narrator is a hand-drawn host in a cardigan, seen mostly from the waist up.

| Section | On screen |
| --- | --- |
| Intro | Title card "DEFAULT ON" in bubble letters. Switch waves from a wall plate. |
| Verse 1 | A cartoon search box. An answer box drops in from above and squashes the links below it. Switch climbs a ladder to reach a tiny "Web" tab. |
| Pre-chorus | Switch pulls himself down to OFF, exhales, and a hand from off-screen flips him back up. He looks around; no one's there. |
| Chorus | Sing-along lyrics with a bouncing ball. On "just a picture," Switch peels off the wall and turns out to be a sticker. |
| Verse 2 | An envelope with eyes reads over a kid's shoulder. A settings menu opens into another menu, then another, like nesting dolls. A TV wakes up at night with a new tile; Switch tries to drag it to a trash can, which turns into a "Hide" box. |
| Verse 3 | Game-show set: "POP QUIZ!" Each question gets a buzzer when Switch answers. The phone screen shows one giant "Continue" button and nothing else. The price tag inflates like a balloon. |
| Bridge | A one-room schoolhouse. A giant hand slides a contract across the desk. Record scratch: the film burns, rewinds, and the Narrator redubs with obviously different mouth shapes. The answer box stays bolted to the top of the screen through the rewind. |
| Final chorus | Cartoon children sing at their desks, each with a toggle switch on the desk flicking itself on in rhythm. |
| Outro | Switch alone on a black wall. Three clicks, nothing changes. A "Summary of this song" box pops up over the frame; its close button runs away from the cursor. Cut to the real generation screen on "An AI did." |

**Running gags**

- **The off-screen hand.** It flips Switch back on in every section. In the outro, it hesitates and doesn't.
- **"Hide" where "Delete" should be.** Every trash can, eraser and X button turns into a "Hide" box.
- **The fleeing close button.** The cursor chases a greyed-out option across the frame in the chorus and the outro.
- **Fine print.** Text scrolls past too fast to read: "on or about October 10," "Search and AI are deeply integrated," "classic plan available upon cancellation."

**Real-world names stay off the cartoon.** The animation uses generic products: a search box, an inbox, a TV, a chat app, a phone. Company names appear only on the fact card at the end and in the description, so the cartoon doesn't imitate anyone's branding.

**Fairness caveats**

- Google's school default is **off** for K-12 student accounts until administrators turn services on. The complaint is that Search can't be had without its built-in AI, not that AI is switched on for students.
- Microsoft has removed Copilot entry points from several Windows apps after the backlash, and Recall now ships disabled by default. The song doesn't claim otherwise.
- The Apple re-enable may have been a bug. The cartoon shows what users saw, not a motive.

**Conflict of interest**

Claude drafted these lyrics, and Claude also ships inside other companies' tools, including Chrome, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace and Slack. Those integrations are, as far as I know, things a person or organisation installs or switches on, but that should be verified rather than taken from me. The last line ("But somebody asked it to") is about this song, not a claim that Anthropic is above the pattern. Send the lyrics to another lab's model for a bias audit, as with Buy It On Margin, and keep Anthropic on the same footing as everyone else if the audit finds a gap. Update, November 6: both audits found that gap. Anthropic's August 2025 consumer-terms pop-up had its training toggle set to On by default. That is now fact card G, shown first, and Switch's last line (V21) asks about it. The human discloses too: right after card G, a real shot (S68) shows his old Google and Microsoft swag, with a caption saying he holds no stock in either and that every claim comes from public reporting. Switch's credit reads "voiced by a child of two ex-Googlers and ex-Microsofties."
