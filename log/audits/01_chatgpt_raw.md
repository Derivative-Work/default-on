## 1. Disclosure: who built me and where the conflict lies

I was built by OpenAI.

OpenAI is not directly accused of wrongdoing in the video. ChatGPT is named as one of the AI models used to audit the creator's work. However, Microsoft is a close OpenAI partner and is explicitly criticised in the video, both over its Copilot shortcut on LG televisions and over the Australian Microsoft 365 pricing controversy.

That creates a potential conflict of interest. I could be unduly favourable toward Microsoft, or conversely overcompensate by being unusually critical of it. There is also a potential perception of competitive bias because Anthropic, the company behind Claude, is named in the video.

I will therefore apply the same standard to every company:

- A claim is VERIFIED only when an opened source supports it as worded.
- A lawsuit or regulator's allegation is not an established fact.
- A company's promise is not proof that a fix shipped.
- A secondary report is not a primary source.
- An inaccessible source is UNVERIFIED, not presumed accurate.
- Developments after the original reporting matter, including developments through November 10, 2026.

One important qualification: I will distinguish between a claim being historically supported and its being accurate as a present-tense claim. Those are not the same thing.

## 2. Claim-by-claim audit

The following is a strict audit, not a general assessment of whether the satire is fair. I opened the supplied sources where possible and checked additional primary material. In particular, I found important developments in the Gmail litigation and the Google schools announcement that materially affect the proposed cards.

Verdict terminology: VERIFIED means supported as worded by an opened source. NEEDS REWORDING means the underlying event is supported but the wording goes too far or omits a material qualification. OUTDATED means a formerly accurate description is no longer safe as a current claim. WRONG means contradicted by the available evidence. UNVERIFIED means I could not establish the claim from an opened source.

### Findings that particularly affect the cards

G — Anthropic's default-on description

The underlying policy change is real and the August 28 date is supported by Anthropic itself. There is, however, a distinction between:

- the existence of a choice to use conversations for training;
- the design of the existing-user acceptance popup;
- the default action of accepting the new terms.

Tom's Guide supports the second and third points. Anthropic's announcement establishes the policy, but does not independently document the popup's visual design or toggle state. I would retain the story but avoid suggesting that every existing user's training preference was silently changed without any prompt.

The human creator's authorship and fact-checking disclosure is a statement of personal responsibility. It cannot be independently verified from these sources, and should not be presented as something this audit has established.

F2 — The lawsuit's procedural history is material

Important correctionThe original card is substantially right about the procedural sequence, but needs a distinction between the first and second dismissal motions.

The opened court order establishes:

- July 7, 2026: the first amended complaint was dismissed with leave to amend.
- August 18, 2026: the second amended complaint was filed.
- September 29, 2026: Google filed another motion to dismiss.

The first dismissal was for lack of adequately pleaded concrete injury. It was not a judicial finding that Google's conduct was lawful or that the alleged settings change never happened.

The second motion was still reported as pending in the November 1 PPC Land article. Its status should not be described as an adjudicated finding.

The original court order is particularly useful because it states that the allegations about October 10 were taken from the plaintiffs' complaint. That makes it inappropriate to write the date as an independently established Google-wide settings change.

F3 — The number of settings

The ModemGuides article actually describes three desktop toggles:

- Gmail, Chat and Meet smart features;
- Google Workspace smart features;
- smart features in other Google products.

The mobile instructions describe two toggles. Thus, the card's two-to-four range is plausible across different guides, but the supplied ModemGuides article does not independently substantiate the upper end of four.

I would not call the existing wording VERIFIED until the other guides underlying that range are named and checked.

F4 — LG's promised fix

The most important technical qualification is that LG described the Copilot tile as a shortcut to Microsoft's website, not an embedded TV application.

This does not eliminate the consumer-choice criticism: a shortcut added through an update and initially lacking a deletion option is still a legitimate subject of the satire.

But the video should not depict the incident as Microsoft secretly installing an autonomous Copilot application into the television. The source attributes the update and the promise of a deletion option to LG.

I also would not put "promised fix unconfirmed" on the card without a date or scope qualification. The general LG deletion guide is not proof that the specific shortcut was fixed.

F5 — Meta's three-app generalisation

The April 2024 Aaj article is a dated report, not a current global product specification.

Meta's current product information confirms that Meta AI is available in the three named apps, but the supplied evidence does not justify a universal statement that every user, in every country, has identical controls.

Muting a conversation is not the same as disabling an AI feature. This distinction is central to the video's theme.

F7 — Australian Microsoft 365

The ACCC release is substantially stronger than the Reuters republication because it gives the actual regulator's allegations and numbers.

The card is right to retain the word "alleges." The price figures and percentages match the regulator's release.

The video should not suggest that a court has found Microsoft guilty of misleading consumers. The proceedings are an allegation until adjudicated or otherwise resolved.

F8–F9 — Google's school policy

Major revision requiredThis is the most important correction in the school section.

The timeline is:

1. November 3, 2026: Google's original policy announcement and the accompanying reporting generated the impression that schools refusing AI would lose Google Search.
2. November 4, 2026: GovTech reported that Google reversed that position.
3. The revised arrangement allows schools to keep Search while turning off standalone Gemini products.
4. Search itself remains AI-enhanced: AI Overviews and AI Mode cannot be separately removed under the described school options.
5. New school controls are expected in early 2027.

The original November 3 claim is historically supportable. It must be narrated as the original policy, not as Google's current position.

The third-box graphic, "Search, chatbot off," is accurate as a depiction of the revised policy. However, the lyric "you keep your search and everything" is too broad: it could reasonably be heard as promising conventional, non-AI Search.

A10's correction should therefore say "you keep Search" rather than "you keep your search and everything."

Also, the phrase "deeply integrated" is a real reported statement, not an invented Google slogan. Its use is fair when explicitly tied to the original November 3 policy.

### Does the video treat one company more harshly than the evidence supports?

My assessment:

- Google: The strongest criticism is supported by actual documentation and reporting, but the Gmail notice and the school-policy timeline need correction.
- Microsoft: The Australian price increase is well documented by the regulator's release. The legal allegation must remain an allegation. The LG shortcut was LG's implementation decision, not evidence that Microsoft installed an embedded television application.
- Meta: The criticism is defensible, but a blanket claim about all three apps, all users and all countries is too broad.
- Apple: The criticism is supported as a report about some users. It should not imply deliberate behaviour by Apple.
- Anthropic: The training-policy change is genuine. The distinction between an opt-in choice and the default acceptance action should be preserved.

I do not see a reason to remove any of the five companies from the satire. The problem is precision, not the choice of subjects.

## 6. Release recommendation

Revise before releaseI would retain the song, its premise, the companies being criticised and the ten-card structure. I would not release the current version unchanged.

The essential edits are:

1. Make A3 unmistakably fictional.
2. Change the LG television wording from application to shortcut.
3. Qualify the Apple incident as affecting some users.
4. Keep the ACCC allegations attributed to the regulator.
5. Update the Gmail litigation history to include the August refiling and September motion.
6. Make the Meta claim explicitly about available controls, not universal inability to disable every integration.
7. Distinguish Google's November 3 school proposal from its November 4 reversal.
8. Replace "you keep your search and everything" with a line that does not imply AI-free Search.
9. Avoid presenting the LG fix as either confirmed or definitively absent across all models.

My strongest recommendation: preserve the cartoon's central argument, but make the factual cards more precise than the song. The cards are where viewers will look to distinguish satire from evidence.

The video's most defensible thesis is not that every AI feature is secretly forced on everyone. It is that, in several documented cases, users have encountered default-enabled features, limited removal options, confusing controls or changes that were difficult to reverse.

That narrower argument is better supported by the evidence, and ultimately makes the satire harder to dismiss.
