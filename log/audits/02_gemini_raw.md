### 3. Flags: Section C, Lyrics, and Comparative Fairness

1. **A3 (Scrolling Fine Print) — HIGH PRIORITY FLAG:**
* **On screen:** *"Smart features were turned on for your account on or about October 10. Some features may remain available."*
* **Why it's misleading:** A viewer will read this scrolling text as a real Google notification or Terms of Service excerpt. In reality, *"on or about October 10, 2025"* is **legal boilerplate from the plaintiffs' complaint** in *Thele v. Google*, and as PPC Land notes, the plaintiffs cited no Google notice or announcement fixing that date (and Google denies flipping the setting at all). Putting the plaintiffs' allegation inside a fake Google system notice makes it look like Google admitted to turning it on Oct 10. Either cut the first sentence or put `[ALLEGED BY PLAINTIFFS, DENIED BY GOOGLE]` in brackets, and use the real Help Center sentence for the second half (*"Even if you turn off smart features in Gmail, Chat, and Meet, some smart features with individual on and off settings are still available"*).


2. **Section C — Maze of Settings Windows ("Smart features: This setting is managed for you"):**
* **Why it could mislead:** In Google Workspace, *"This setting is managed by your administrator"* appears on **work or school accounts**, not personal consumer Gmail accounts (where the user *can* uncheck the box). Since A2/A3/A4 is about the consumer class action (*Thele v. Google*), showing *"Smart features: This setting is managed for you"* inside the consumer settings maze could lead a viewer to believe Google locked the toggle on personal Gmail accounts.
* **Fix:** Change that window label to actual consumer UI text from the complaint/ModemGuides, e.g., **"Manage Workspace smart feature settings"** or **"Turn on smart features in Gmail, Chat, and Meet"**.


3. **A9 / A10 / F8 / F9 — Framing Google's Nov. 3, 2026 School Policy as a "Reversal" on the Standalone Chatbot:**
* **Fairness / accuracy issue:** The two-scene structure in A9 and A10 implies that on Nov. 3, 2026, Google initially told schools *"you must enable the Gemini chatbot to keep Google Search,"* and then backed down ("reversal") to let schools turn off the Gemini chatbot.
* **What the primary source actually shows:** I opened Chris Phillips's original Nov. 3, 2026 Google blog post ([Designing responsible AI for learning](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-learning-era-updates-2026/)). From the start, it stated: *"we are now updating our approach to controls for Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search. Access to these products will be entirely off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on."* The controversy was never that schools had to turn on the standalone Gemini app to get Search; the controversy was (and still is!) that **Google Search itself contains AI Overviews, AI Mode, or Guided Learning**, which cannot be turned off inside Search. When headlines wrote "Schools that reject AI will lose Search," Google posted on X on Nov. 3 to clarify that standalone Gemini and Search have separate toggles—while holding firm that Search itself always includes AI.
* **Fix:** Calling it a **"clarification"** rather than a **"reversal"** (in A10, A11, and F9) is fairer to the primary record and actually makes the satire's real point sharper: Google *didn't* back down on forcing AI into school Search, and as of early 2027, both Gemini and Search will actually default to **OFF** for K–12 until an admin flips them on.


4. **Card F9 — August 2026 K–12 Default Context:**
* **Nuance check:** Common Sense Media (opened) notes that the August 2026 default-on change for the "Gemini" tab in Google Classroom *"applies only to schools that had already given students access to the Gemini app or Gemini Notebook."* F9 says *"for many K-12 student accounts,"* which matches Common Sense Media's first sentence verbatim, so it passes—just be sure to add the Oct. 21, 2026 Hawley/Blumenthal (or Nov. 9, 2026 Markey) Senate letter URL to the source list so the *"drawing a Senate letter"* claim is backed by a listed source.


5. **Card G — "with audits by Gemini and ChatGPT":**
* **Flag:** Until you incorporate the fixes from this audit (especially A3, F4's Dec 1, 2026 retirement, and the missing Senate letter source in F9), stating on-screen that the video was audited by Gemini could mislead viewers into thinking every claim as originally drafted received a clean bill of health.


6. **All other jokes in Section C (Cookie, Broccoli "CHEF'S PICK" / "YOU CHOSE THIS", Earmuffs on the glowing ring, Enormous "Continue" button, "deeply integrated" bolted plate, runaway close button):**
* **Verdict:** **PASS.** All of these read clearly as 1970s cartoon visual gags and fair satirical commentary on dark patterns and default settings; no reasonable viewer would mistake them for literal product screenshots.
